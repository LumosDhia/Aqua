1. Purpose and scope
This report describes how every part of the AquaFarm plugs into one custom PCB: pin
assignments, connectors, the small circuits that live on the board, power, and layout rules.
It follows the current firmware (aquafarm_standalone.ino), so the board works with the
existing code without changes. The ESP32 dev board sits in sockets on the PCB so it can
be replaced, and every off-board part (sensors, pump, display, power) connects through a
labelled connector.


2. Board architecture
       ESP32 dev board in two female header rows (not soldered).
       5 V input on connector J1 feeds the 5 V rail. The ESP32 board's regulator makes the 3.3
       V rail.
       Pump switch on the board: a 5 V relay (K1) driven by a transistor.
       Status parts on the board: green, blue and red LEDs, and a buzzer with its own driver.
       Connectors for the pump, soil sensor, rain sensor, ultrasonic sensor, DHT11 and the
       round display.
       The ECHO level divider and the sensor filter capacitors sit on the board, close to the
       ESP32.

Do not use GPIO 0, 2 and 12 (boot strapping), 6 to 11 (flash) or 1 and 3 (serial). GPIO 13, 14,
16 and 17 are free and go to the spare header J8 (16 and 17 are not available on boards with
PSRAM).


4. Power design
       Input: regulated 5 V, 2 A, on J1. A 1.5 A resettable fuse and a series Schottky diode
       (1N5819) for reverse-polarity protection are optional.
       The 5 V rail feeds the ESP32 VIN pin, the relay coil, the HC-SR04, the buzzer and the
       pump (through the relay contacts).
       The 3.3 V rail comes from the ESP32 board's 3V3 pin and feeds the soil sensor, rain
       sensor, DHT11 and display.
       Put a 470 to 1000 µF electrolytic capacitor (10 V or more) right at J1, and a 10 µF plus
       100 nF pair next to the ESP32 VIN pin. They absorb the pump's start-up surge, which
       can otherwise cause brownout resets.
       Use a star ground: the pump and relay return current goes straight back to the J1
       ground, never through the ESP32 or sensor ground traces.

5. Circuit blocks

5.1 Pump and relay
Parts: K1 5 V relay (for example SRD-05VDC-SL-C), Q1 NPN transistor (BC337 or 2N2222),
R1 1 kΩ, R2 10 kΩ, D4 and D5 1N4007, C1 100 nF.

      GPIO26 goes through R1 to the base of Q1. R2 pulls the base to GND, so the relay stays
      off while the ESP32 boots or resets.
      The Q1 emitter goes to GND and the collector to one side of the relay coil. The other
      side of the coil goes to +5 V.
      D4 sits across the relay coil, with the stripe (cathode) on the +5 V side.
      Relay contacts: COM to +5 V, NO to J2 pin 1 (PUMP+). J2 pin 2 (PUMP-) goes to GND.
      Leave NC unconnected. Nothing may join NC to NO, because that bypasses the relay
      and keeps the pump running.
      D5 sits across J2 (the pump terminals), stripe toward PUMP+, with C1 in parallel. D5
      absorbs the voltage spike when the relay opens.

The firmware sets GPIO26 high to switch the pump on, which matches this circuit.

5.2 Ultrasonic sensor (HC-SR04)
      J5 carries 5 V, TRIG, ECHO and GND. TRIG goes straight to GPIO27.
      The ECHO signal is 5 V and must be reduced for the ESP32. R3 (1 kΩ) goes from J5
      ECHO to a middle node, R4 (2 kΩ) from that node to GND, and the node goes to
      GPIO18. The result is 5 V × 2/3 = 3.3 V.
      If you do not have a 2 kΩ resistor, use two 1 kΩ resistors in series. Place the divider
      close to GPIO18.


5.3 Soil and rain sensors
      Both run from 3.3 V and use analog inputs on ADC1 (GPIO34 and GPIO35), which keep
      working while Wi-Fi is on.
      For the soil sensor, wire only the AOUT pin. The DO pin gives just 0 or 4095 and is not
      used. Print AOUT on the silkscreen next to J3 pin 3.
      Add a 100 nF capacitor from each analog line to GND, at the ESP32 end. Keep the
      cable to the rain sensor short, since long cables pick up noise.


5.4 DHT11
      J6 carries 3V3, DATA and GND. R5 (10 kΩ) pulls DATA up to 3V3. It does no harm if the
      module already has its own pull-up.


5.5 Round display (GC9A01)
      J7 follows the module's pin order: RST, CS, DC, SDA, SCL, GND, VCC. VCC goes to 3.3
      V, and the backlight is always on, since the module has no backlight pin.
      SDA and SCL are SPI data and clock, not I2C. Keep these traces short and away from
      the relay and pump traces.


5.6 LEDs and buzzer
      D1 (green), D2 (blue) and D3 (red) each connect from their GPIO through a 330 Ω
      resistor to the LED, then to GND.
      Buzzer driver: GPIO23 goes through R6 (1 kΩ) to the base of Q2 (BC337). R7 (10 kΩ)
      pulls the base to GND. The Q2 emitter goes to GND, the buzzer's + pin to +5 V and its -
      pin to the Q2 collector. Use an active buzzer.

7. Layout guidelines
       Two-layer board, 1.6 mm FR4, 1 oz copper, with a ground pour on both sides and
       stitching vias.
       Pump path (J1, relay contacts, J2): traces at least 0.8 mm wide. Signal traces: 0.25 mm.
       Put the relay, J1, J2 and the bulk capacitor together on one board edge, away from the
       ESP32 antenna and the analog lines.
       Let the ESP32 antenna end overhang the board edge or keep copper out of the area
       under it.
       Place the 100 nF analog capacitors and the ECHO divider right next to their ESP32
       pins.
       Mark diode polarity (stripe), connector pin 1, and 'NC: leave empty' on the silkscreen.
       Add M3 mounting holes in the corners.

9. Bring-up and test procedure
   1. Before ordering, print the board at 1:1 on paper and place the real ESP32 board, relay
      and connectors on it to check the fit.




                                                                                                Page 6 of 7
AquaFarm: Custom PCB Wiring Report




   2. Solder the board without the ESP32 and the relay. Check for a short between +5 V and
      GND with a multimeter.
   3. Apply 5 V and check the 5 V rail and the ESP32 socket pins. Then plug in the ESP32
      and the relay.
   4. Upload the sketch and open the serial monitor. There should be no brownout message
      at boot.
   5. With the pump disconnected, press Water now in Manual mode. The relay should
      click, and COM to NO should conduct only while it is on.
   6. Connect the pump. It must stay off until commanded, then run for the chosen time
      and stop.
   7. Calibrate the soil sensor (SOIL_DRY and SOIL_WET from the raw value in dry air and in
      water) and the tank (FULL_CM and EMPTY_CM).


10. Open points
      Display CS (GPIO15) and DC (GPIO19) are assumed from the sketch. Confirm them
      against the real wiring before ordering the board.
      Check the pin order printed on the display module against the J7 order above.
      The current figures are estimates. Measure the pump's running and start-up current
      to confirm the 2 A supply.
      The soil sensor must be wired from its AOUT pin, never DO.
